Are You a Woman-Owned Business? Here's Why Women Helping Women Is Your Fastest Path Forward

If you're a woman-owned business owner, the single most reliable thing you can do to move faster is find other women who will actually help you — and in Dallas, a surprising amount of that help is free. Women helping women isn't a nice sentiment. It's a measurable advantage, and there's a study to prove it.

Start with the numbers most of us already carry around, whether we've said them out loud or not.

The stats every woman-owned business owner already feels in her gut

Women own 39.2% of all U.S. businesses and employ 12.9 million people — yet we generate only 6.2% of total firm revenue (Wells Fargo/WIPP 2025 Impact of Women-Owned Businesses Report). Nearly 40% of the businesses, 6% of the revenue.

At the current pace, it would take an estimated 120 years to close that revenue gap.

A few more, because you already know these:

  1. Women launched 49% of all new U.S. businesses last year — up from 29% in 2019 (Gusto 2025 New Business Formation Report). We're starting companies faster than ever.
  2. Women receive less than 5% of small business lending, despite owning majority stakes in at least 35% of employer firms (Lendio 2024). We start them, then get starved of the capital to grow them.
  3. Only about 11% of investing partners at U.S. venture capital firms are women, and nearly 75% of VC firms have zero women investing partners at all (Forbes/Harvard Kennedy School). Over 75% of men who invest, invest in other men. So the money mostly flows man-to-man in a closed loop. That's not a conspiracy. It's just a room, and most of us aren't in it.

Through HER Texas, a network that works specifically to grow the economic value of women, I learned that closing that revenue gap would add $10.2 trillion annually to the U.S. economy (Wells Fargo/WIPP 2025). We are not asking for a favor. We are the growth strategy nobody's funding.

Women helping women isn't sentimental — it's a documented performance advantage

Now the good news, and it's better than any motivational quote we use to keep ourselves going. A peer-reviewed 2024 study from INFORMS and the Becker Friedman Institute at the University of Chicago found that female entrepreneurs guided by female mentors showed measurably better business outcomes than those mentored by men. The researchers called gender-matched mentorship "a promising strategy" for clearing the systemic barriers women face.

They also noted something dryly funny: male mentors improved their results when they adopted the engagement styles more common among female mentors. So even the fix for cross-gender mentoring is — be more like the women.

A few more, because this is also common knowledge:

  1. Companies in the top quartile for gender diversity are 25% more likely to outperform financially (McKinsey, via Women Business Collaborative).
  2. Women held only 8.7% of CEO roles as of 2024 — so the outperformance is happening without us at the top, not because we're absent.
  3. Gender parity in entrepreneurship could add $5–6 trillion to the global economy, and the Women Entrepreneurs Finance Initiative names training and mentorship — especially the kind that addresses confidence — as one of the most critical levers for scaling women-owned businesses.

The pattern is boring in its consistency: when women build the room instead of waiting for a seat in someone else's, everyone's numbers go up.

Free programs for women-owned businesses in Dallas that are actually worth your time

Before you spend a dollar, spend a few afternoons looking into what's already available for your business. Dallas has real, funded, free-to-you resources, and most women-owned business owners here don't use half of them.

Texas Woman's University — Center for Women Entrepreneurs (CWE). Part of the Jane Nelson Institute for Women's Leadership, CWE has awarded more than $2 million to 400+ women entrepreneurs since 2015 and run over 300 training sessions. Their StartHER cohort fast-tracks early-stage women-owned businesses, and in October 2025 they awarded 10 micro-grants to North Texas founders, including two in Dallas. Start at twu.edu.

NAWBO Dallas/Fort Worth. The regional chapter of the National Association of Women Business Owners tracks funding, policy, and networking for North Texas women. Worth knowing right now: the Acting Texas Comptroller announced a temporary freeze on Texas HUB (Historically Underutilized Business) certifications in late 2025 — a policy change that directly affects women-owned certification in the state, and exactly the kind of thing NAWBO flags before it blindsides you. Find them at nawbo.org/dallas-ft-worth.

Dallas SCORE and the Dallas Metropolitan SBDC. Free mentoring, free workshops, free one-on-one advising, plus government contracting support — both partially funded through SBA cooperative agreements. This is help your tax dollars already paid for. score.org/dallas and the Dallas SBDC.

SBA Women-Owned Small Business (WOSB) certification. The federal government's goal is to award at least 5% of all federal contracting dollars to women-owned small businesses each year. The SBA's Dallas/Fort Worth District Office serves 72 Texas counties and offers free counseling and certification help. If federal contracts are anywhere in your future, this is the free door.

Use these. They're the doorway, not the room.
